# Plan: from handwriting drill to a Duolingo-style Mandarin course

Status: the five decisions in the last section are settled. Phases 1 to 4
are built. Phase 0 is done for Book 1: all fifteen lessons are transcribed
from the printed textbook (word lists, glosses, grammar points, page numbers
and coverage-valid sentences), and `spine.verified` is true. Books 2 to 4
are the remaining Phase 0 work. Audio was last, by the reasoning under the
phase table, and depends on a voice the phone provides.

## 1. Goal

Keep what the app does well (graded handwriting, offline, no accounts, FSRS)
and wrap it in a Duolingo-style course that actually teaches Mandarin: words,
sentences, grammar, listening, tones. Lessons follow an official textbook so
each unit has a topic, a vocabulary list, and grammar points, and the app can
point at the book chapter to read alongside.

## 2. What Duolingo does that we copy, skip, or beat

This section is about *features*. For the density and ordering of exposures
inside one lesson -- how often a new word must come back, and where -- see
`docs/duolingo-teaching-pattern.md`, which measures what our generator actually
produces and specifies what it should.

Copy (verified against Duolingo's blog, research papers, and course trackers):

- Path: section → unit → levels → lessons of ~15 exercises each. Unit ends in
  a review. Each unit has a guidebook (key phrases + grammar notes).
- Small fixed set of exercise types, with a per-lesson cap on any one type.
- Missed exercises re-queue inside the same lesson. Lesson ends only when every
  item has been answered correctly once.
- Long-term review is a separate forgetting model driving a Practice hub
  (Mistakes, Words, Listen). We already have FSRS for this.
- Motivation: XP per lesson, daily goal ring, streak with a freeze, end-of-lesson
  stats (accuracy, time).
- Words are shown in sentences from day one, built only from vocabulary already
  taught.

Skip (solo, offline, no server):

- Hearts, gems, leagues, friend streaks, quests.
- Speaking exercises. iOS speech recognition needs the network and cannot score
  tones reliably. Reconsider later.

Beat (the most repeated criticisms of Duolingo Chinese):

- No handwriting or stroke order. We already grade strokes.
- Thin grammar. Every unit gets a written grammar tip with pattern + examples.
- Tones barely taught. Add a tone-identification exercise.
- Meaning never taught explicitly. Keep our "meet it, build it, recognise it"
  intro before any drill.

## 3. Curriculum spine

Recommendation: **HSK Standard Course (标准教程, BLCU Press), Books 1 to 3**.
It is the official HSK-aligned textbook. Books 1 and 2 have 15 lessons each,
Book 3 has 20. Fifty lessons cover HSK 1 to 3 (2.0 bands), roughly 600 words.

What we take from the book, and why it is safe:

- Lesson order, lesson topic, which words and grammar points each lesson
  introduces. These are facts, not protected expression.
- Nothing else. No dialogues, no exercise text, no illustrations.

Where the lesson facts come from:

- Chinese Grammar Wiki reference pages `Reference:HSK_Standard_Course_1..3`
  map each lesson to its grammar points (CC BY-NC-SA 3.0, attribute AllSet
  Learning). A GitHub mirror exists at `ivankra/asg`.
- Per-lesson word lists are compiled from public vocabulary lists and verified
  against the owner's copy of the book. This is a data-entry task, about 50
  lessons × 15 to 30 words.
- Word data (pinyin, meaning, HSK 2.0 and 3.0 bands, frequency) already comes
  from `complete-hsk-vocabulary` (MIT), which is cached in `tools/cache`.

Grammar explanations: written by us, one short tip per grammar point, pattern
plus two or three example sentences. Cross-checked against the Grammar Wiki.

Sentences: each unit needs about 20 to 30 sentences built only from words taught
so far. Two sources, in order:

1. Tatoeba zh-en pairs (CC BY 2.0 FR) filtered to sentences whose every word is
   already taught. Expect sparse coverage in early units.
2. Sentences authored at build time (by the owner, or generated by a model and
   reviewed by the owner), stored in JSON. The runtime stays free of AI calls.

Alternative considered: use the HSK 3.0 official standard alone (word, character,
and grammar lists in `krmanik/HSK-3.0`). Cleaner licensing, but no lesson
topics and no textbook to read alongside. We use it only to tag levels.

## 4. How writing fits a word-ordered course

The README argues, correctly, that teaching 谢 (17 strokes) in lesson 2 is bad
for handwriting. Duolingo does not have this problem because it never teaches
writing. Resolution: two tracks per unit.

- **Reading track** follows the textbook. Lesson 2 teaches 谢谢 for
  recognition, listening, and use in sentences.
- **Writing track** keeps the existing component-dependency order, but scoped to
  the characters of the current and earlier units. A character's writing card
  unlocks when its components are met. Components not in the HSK word list
  (讠, 身, 寸 for 谢) appear as short "building block" cards: meet, trace, done.
  The writing track lags the reading track by a unit or two, by design.

## 5. Exercise types

Generated at runtime from unit data, no hand-authored exercises.

| type | prompt → answer | source data |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Meet a word | word, pinyin, meaning, audio, characters it uses | word |
| Select | audio or pinyin → pick the hanzi (4 tiles); or hanzi → pick meaning | word |
| Match pairs | 5 hanzi ↔ 5 pinyin, or hanzi ↔ meaning | words in unit |
| Word bank, EN → ZH | English → tap tiles to build the sentence | sentence tokens + distractors |
| Word bank, ZH → EN | Chinese → tap English tiles | sentence, accepted answers |
| Listen | audio → pick or build the sentence | sentence + TTS |
| Fill the blank | sentence with a hole, grammar-targeted → pick the word | sentence + grammar tag |
| Tone | hear a syllable → tap tone 1 to 4 (or neutral) | word pinyin |
| Write | meaning + pinyin → write the character (existing) | stroke data |
| Write the word | two or three characters in sequence | stroke data |

Listen and Tone are the only two types that cannot be built without sound, so
they are generated only when a Mandarin voice is available. Meet and Select
take audio as an addition, not a requirement: they read from pinyin, and speak
as well where they can.

Grading rules: word bank compares token sequences against an accepted list.
Listen and select are exact. Tone and select are exact. Writing keeps the
current Easy/Good/Hard/Again mapping.

Lesson recipe: 15 exercises. First lesson of a unit is mostly Meet + Select +
Match. Later levels shift toward word bank, listen, cloze, and write. No type
appears more than 4 times per lesson.

## 6. Audio

Last phase, by choice. See the note under the phase table for why. Nothing
before it may assume sound exists. Built: see 9d.

Primary: `speechSynthesis` with a zh-CN voice. iOS voices run on device, so it
works offline once a Chinese voice is installed in Settings → Accessibility →
Spoken Content. Known limits: voice list may be empty in a PWA, playback must be
triggered by a tap, and utterances can die when the app is backgrounded. Design
every audio exercise with a visible replay button and a "show pinyin" fallback.

Later option: bundle generated audio per word (paid-tier Azure or Google TTS,
generated once at build time, about 600 clips). Not in scope for the first pass.
Avoid Forvo (terms forbid bundling) and edge-tts (unsanctioned service).

## 7. Data model

Replace `data/curriculum.json` with `data/course.json` plus per-unit bundles.

```
course.json
  sections[]        { n, title, units: [unitIds] }          Book 1, Book 2, Book 3
  units[]           { id, book, lesson, title, topic,
                      words: [wordIds], grammar: [grammarIds],
                      sentences: [sentenceIds],
                      writing: [charIds in dependency order],
                      levels: 4 }                            levels × lessons per unit
  words{}           { id, word, pinyin, tones[], meaning, pos, chars[], hsk2, hsk3 }
  grammar{}         { id, title, pattern, tip, examples: [sentenceIds], wikiRef }
  sentences{}       { id, zh, tokens[], pinyin, en, accepted[], grammar[], unit }
  chars{}           { char, pinyin, meaning, strokes, radical, hint, parts[],
                      examples[], unit, block }               writing cards
data/units/NN.json  strokes for that unit's writing track
```

Progress store bumps to `v: 2` with a migration that keeps existing `char:w`
FSRS cards. New FSRS item kinds: `word:r` (read), `sent:b` (build), `char:w`
(unchanged), `word:w` (write the whole word), and `word:l` (listen) once
Phase 4 lands. Lesson completion, XP,
streak, and daily goal live beside `cards`.

Known data bug this fixes: card meanings currently take the first HSK entry for
a character, so 也 shows "surname Ye" and pinyin "Yě". Meanings should come from
the word list, and single-character cards should prefer the common gloss.

## 8. Screens

1. **Path**: sections stacked, units as nodes with 4 level dots, current unit
   highlighted. Tap a node to start the next lesson; long-press for the
   guidebook. Streak and daily goal ring in the header.
2. **Guidebook**: unit topic, textbook reference ("Book 1, Lesson 4"), word
   list, grammar tips with examples. Per-word audio arrives in Phase 4.
3. **Lesson**: progress bar, one exercise at a time, immediate feedback strip
   (green or red, correct answer shown), missed items re-queued. Existing
   session screen becomes one exercise type inside this runner.
4. **Lesson summary**: XP, accuracy, time, items to review, "practice mistakes".
5. **Practice hub**: Mistakes, Words due (FSRS), Writing due. The current deck
   screen becomes Writing due. A Listening lane joins them in Phase 4.
6. **Settings**: existing items, plus daily XP goal, pinyin display
   (always, on tap, never), tone marks vs numbers. The voice test button
   arrived with Phase 4; the two display settings are still open.

Phase 1 can start against Book 1, Lessons 1 to 3 as fixture data.

Audio is deliberately last. It is the one part of the app that depends on
something we do not control — whether iOS has a zh-CN voice installed and
whether it survives a PWA — so everything that works without it gets built
first. Without a voice the course is silent and fully playable: pinyin is
always on screen, and the two exercise types that need sound are simply not
generated.

## 9a. What Phase 2 settled

- **A unit's writing track unlocks when a lesson of that unit is played**, not
  when the unit is finished. That gives the lag the plan asked for — one unit,
  not two — while keeping the writing queue fed from day one.
- **Building blocks get a shorter card**: meet it, see the characters it turns
  up inside, trace it. There is no recognise step, because a block is never a
  word and there is nothing to recognise it among. Which characters are blocks
  is decided at build time, from whether any word in any book uses them.
- **Write-the-word is a card in the writing session**, not an exercise in the
  lesson runner. It uses one grid and steps through the characters in sequence,
  which fits a phone; three grids side by side does not. Its FSRS card is
  `w:<word>:w`, so it schedules independently of the word's reading card.
- **A word card only appears once every character in it has a writing card**,
  so the sequence is never the first time you meet a shape.
- **The Practice hub is three lanes over the one scheduler** — Mistakes (FSRS
  relearning), Words and sentences (due), Writing. Practice earns XP but never
  moves the path along.
- **The old HSK-band deck is not deleted.** `data/curriculum.json` stays as the
  stroke source for characters the course does not carry, and cards started in
  it keep coming up for review.

## 9b. What the teaching rework settled

Measured, specified and implemented in `docs/duolingo-teaching-pattern.md`;
summarised here because it changed the shape of a unit.

- **A unit's words are batched three to a teaching level**, written into
  `course.json` as `unit.intro`, with two review levels after the last teaching
  level. `unit.levels` is no longer the same for every unit.
- **Which words a level introduces is data, not store state.** The generator
  used to introduce whatever it had not seen before, which made replaying a
  level teach nothing.
- **The lesson generator is word-centric.** It schedules each new word's
  retrievals first — one at a lag of two to four exercises, one at five or more,
  recognition before production — and fills what is left by type under the
  existing cap.
- **`meet` is an exercise, not a slide**, and no longer grades the word `Good`
  for having been looked at.
- **A hint exists in the reading track and costs a grade**, matching what the
  writing cards already did.
- **The store counts lifetime retrievals per card** (`v: 3`) and review slots
  prefer the least-practised eligible word.
- **Nothing shows up before it is taught**, per level and per slot. The old
  generator drew distractors from the whole unit.

## 9c. What Phase 3 settled

- **The guidebook opens from a button beside the path node**, not a long
  press. iOS swallows long presses on a page that has disabled the touch
  callout, and a visible control is one fewer thing to discover. It is
  readable for any unit that has content, locked or not, and its one button
  does whatever tapping the node would.
- **A hard replay is what a finished unit plays.** It is the wide review
  recipe with production first, five distractors instead of three, five spare
  tiles instead of three, and no hint button. It earns 5 XP over a lesson and
  never calls `completeLevel`. The difficulty lives in a module flag inside
  the generator, set for the one synchronous build and cleared after, because
  it is read deep inside the makers.
- **The service worker reads its unit bundles off `course.json`** at install
  instead of listing them, so adding a unit to the spine needs only the
  `VERSION` bump. The legacy deck's stroke bundles are no longer precached;
  they are cached the first time a card from that deck comes up.
- **`check.mjs` now covers the worker**: every precached file exists, every
  file the shell references is precached, and the unit list is not hard-coded.

## 9d. What Phase 4 settled

- **One module owns the voice.** `js/audio.js` reads the system voice list
  lazily, keeps Mandarin voices only (mainland first, Cantonese excluded),
  and exposes `available()`, `speak()` and `onChange()`. Reading lazily is
  what lets the checks drop a stub in and run the generator's second shape.
- **The listening card is the word's, `word:l`.** Every audio exercise grades
  it: hearing the word and picking it, naming a tone in it, and hearing a
  sentence that uses it (which grades every word in the sentence). Nothing
  starts the card early; the first listening exercise a word appears in
  creates it, so the Listening lane fills only with words that have been
  heard.
- **By ear is recognition.** In a teaching level the sound-to-hanzi rung of
  the ladder is played by ear half the time when there is a voice. It is the
  same recognition without the pinyin crutch, so the teaching invariants
  (recognised before produced, retrievals inside the window) hold with or
  without sound, and `check.mjs` runs them both ways.
- **Tone questions dodge sandhi.** A third tone before a third tone, and 不
  or 一 inside a word, are said differently from the way the dictionary
  writes them, so those syllables are never asked. The five tiles are the
  syllable written under each tone rather than the words "first" to
  "fourth".
- **The pinyin fallback is a hint and is priced like one**, but unlike a
  hint it stays on offer in a hard replay: it is the recovery path when the
  voice has died, not help with the answer.
- **The recipes name the audio types and the generator skips them in
  silence**, so a review level or hard replay has one shape, with the audio
  slots filled by the next type in the loop when there is no voice.

## 11. Decisions taken

1. **Spine: HSK Standard Course, Books 1 to 3.** `tools/spine/book-N.json` holds
   one file per book. Book 1 exists with all fifteen lesson titles, topics and
   page numbers; Lessons 1 to 3 also carry word lists, grammar tips and
   sentences. Lessons 4 to 15 are titles only, and build as units marked
   `ready: false`, which the path shows greyed out.
2. **Sentences: authored at build time, owner reviews.** They live in the spine
   file, and `tools/check.mjs` fails the build if any sentence uses a word its
   unit has not taught yet.
3. **Audio: system TTS only, and last.** It moved to Phase 4. Nothing in
   Phases 1 to 3 depends on it.
4. **Textbook: owned.** The path and guidebook name the book and lesson, and
   every unit carries a `page`.
5. **First milestone: Book 1.** Book 2 and 3 spine files come later; the builder
   picks up any `book-N.json` it finds.

Remaining Phase 0 work:

- Book 1 is fully transcribed from the printed textbook (`HSK标准教程 1`,
  北京语言大学出版社) and `spine.verified` is true. The source PDFs live under
  `docs/private/` (gitignored — copyrighted); facts only (word lists, order,
  grammar points, page numbers) are taken into `tools/spine/book-1.json`.
- Books 2 to 4 still need their word lists, grammar points and sentences
  transcribed the same way. The PDFs are already in `docs/private/`.
